About Tyler Ziegler

Locksmith, hardware technologist, and prairie maker operating out of Brandon and Western Manitoba.

DISPATCHED: Tuesday, Oct 20, 2026
•
READ TIME: 3 min (571 words)

Hello, I’m Tyler Ziegler.

I am an independent locksmith, physical security researcher, and hardware technologist living and operating in Western Manitoba (Westman).

My work sits at the unique cross-section between century-old pin-tumbler mechanical lock craft, harsh prairie winter survival, and cutting-edge digital hardware telemetry. Whether I am decoding a 6-pin high-security Medeco cylinder in my Brandon workshop, servicing a frozen master-key system at an ag facility 100 kilometers out in a -38°C blizzard, or programming 915 MHz LoRa wireless nodes to monitor remote cattle gates, my goal is the same: honest, robust, uncompromised security for rural communities.

The Philosophy: High Tech Meets Prairie Grit

A lot of modern “smart security” sold today relies on flimsy Wi-Fi cloud subscriptions, thin stamped zinc housings, and fragile consumer electronics that brick the first time an Arctic cold snap blows through Manitoba.

I believe in an older, more durable standard:

  1. Physical Integrity First: High-security mechanical cylinders (Medeco, Abloy Protec2, Mul-T-Lock, Assa Twin) with hardened anti-drill pins, solid brass plugs, and unpickable sidebars are the foundation of real protection.
  2. Cold-Weather Engineering: If a lock or electronic access controller can’t withstand -45°C windchills, torrential prairie dust, and sudden spring condensation, it doesn’t belong in rural Manitoba.
  3. Open Hardware & Sovereign Telemetry: No mandatory corporate cloud subscriptions for farm security. We build with open standard protocols (LoRaWAN, MQTT, local encrypted micro-servers) that keep telemetry operational even when the cellular grid or internet goes down.
  4. Artisan Bench Craft: A deep respect for metal, wood, and leather. Every custom pick, plug follower, and tension tool machined in our workshop is made to last a lifetime.

The Mobile Field Unit

When a dispatch comes in from Virden, Minnedosa, Dauphin, Neepawa, or Souris, the work happens on site.

Our mobile workshop is built inside a high-roof Ford Transit 350 AWD:

  • Fully spray-foam insulated with a dedicated 4kW auxiliary diesel heater capable of maintaining +18°C inside the van while outside temperatures plummet below -40°C.
  • 400Ah LiFePO4 battery bank paired with a 2000W pure sine wave inverter, solar roof panels, and secondary alternator charging.
  • Onboard key duplication, cylinder pinning station, safe drilling rig, and lock opening picks.
  • Starlink satellite terminal for continuous high-speed data, GPS routing, and encrypted dispatch telemetry across dead cellular zones.
